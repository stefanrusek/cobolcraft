## Lane 002-lane-perfpresent (T59800+)

Operator report (try17): 4 fps in a taiga village with the machine load near 10. A macOS `sample` of the running game (scratchpad/try17-4fps.sample, 2161 main-thread samples at 1 ms) shows 66 percent of the main thread inside `cc_vk_frame_end`: 722 samples in `vkQueueSubmit` (714 of them MoltenVK encoding the Metal commands on the calling thread, 647 of those blocked in `-[CAMetalLayer nextDrawable]` on a dispatch semaphore, called from `MVKCmdBeginRenderPass::encode` -> `populateMTLRenderPassAttachmentDescriptor` -> `MVKPresentableSwapchainImage::getCAMetalDrawable`: MoltenVK takes the drawable lazily at encode time, not in `vkAcquireNextImageKHR`, which costs 6 samples) and 700 in `vkWaitForFences` (the GPU finishing the frame). The rest: 503 in the COBOL frame step, 55 recording draws (`cc_vk_draw`), 78 in `cc_vk_texture_update_end` (`vkQueueWaitIdle`), 34 in `cc_vk_texture_update` (staging `vkAllocateMemory`), 18 in `cc_vk_mesh` (`vkAllocateMemory`), 21 in `cc_vk_frame_begin`, 9 in `vkQueuePresentKHR`. Draw encoding inside submit is about 60 samples (`MVKCmdDraw::encode` 47, push constants and descriptor binding about 13). Nothing else is large.

Cause in the code (before this lane): `cc_vk_frame_end` = submit + wait for the frame's fence + present, one command buffer, one acquire semaphore, one fence (the header said "one frame in flight"), so each frame was the strict sequence COBOL, then Metal encoding (blocked until the compositor releases a drawable), then the GPU, then the next COBOL step; the three measured parts (about 90, 75 and 80 ms a frame, derived from the sample over about 8.8 frames) add up to the 4 fps. `vkQueueWaitIdle` in the batched animated-texture upload (every frame with an animated sprite) would also have emptied any pipeline. The swapchain was already 3 images (`minImageCount` 2 + 1 on MoltenVK), FIFO, `preTransform` current, opaque; the layer settings (displaySyncEnabled, maximumDrawableCount, framebufferOnly) are MoltenVK's from the present mode and image count.
