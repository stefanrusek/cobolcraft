## Lane 002-lane-bugvlag (T43200-T43399; operator report on try15: "much smoother, but not in the village, wow that is laggy")

Measured, not guessed. Benchmarks (release build, CPU time of the process by C clock(), so a loaded machine moves them little; base = try15 6789c8b5): `core/tests/test_bench_village.cob` (CORE-TICK phase by phase, and with CC_VB_MOBS=1 MOB-STEP summed by species; superflat world seed 12345, the village start at chunk (-13, 25), view 8, creative player standing on the village, 11 villagers, 1 iron golem, 3 cats, 2 cows, 2 pigs; the field is a place 14 chunks from any village start; CC_VB_BENCH=1 to measure, without it a 20 tick smoke) and `tests/convergence/bench_village_client.cob` (the real client frame through the single-player host: standard world seed 12345, `/tp` to the village at chunk (-9, -52) or to a field at chunk (9, -78), view 4; built by hand, not an acc_ test).

Findings (sampling of the release bench with `sample`, then the per-species table): the two largest costs were not village specific but the village made the first one three times worse: (1) the villagers' POI index scan (`PXI-SCAN`: one 16 x 16 x 16 section a call, every call a `PXI-KIND-OF`, which does `PXI-INIT`, two chunk lookups and the coordinate arithmetic for each of its 4096 cells: 8 ms a section; each villager scans 24 sections of each of 49 chunks round it, one section per 5 ticks: the scan was still running 1500 ticks (75 s) after arriving in the base build, and it held 35 percent of the samples of a village tick); (2) the natural spawner (`MS6-SPAWN-CHUNK`) found the top of the column of every candidate chunk and category by calling `WORLD-GET-BLOCK` for each cell from the top of the world down (380 calls in a flat world, a full lookup each): 24 to 27 ms of every tick with the player alone in a field, 289 chunks near the player dealt over 20 ticks. With those two gone the rest of a villager's tick is generic mob work: libcob's `FUNCTION ATAN` in `MOB-YAW-TOWARD` (15 percent of the settled village), the cramming scan of the environment tick (a walk over 1024 entity rows with `STDMOB-INDEX` and `FUNCTION ABS`, on one tick in four for each mob: 30 percent of `M182-ENV-TICK`), and `GI-SOLID-CELL` through `ENTITY-BOX-HITS` (a second chunk lookup for each cell of a mob's box). Not found to matter: packet volume (31 bytes a tick to the player from a village, mostly the movement packets of the villagers), block entity ticks (5 us), random ticks and crops (0.8 ms, the same in the field), the iron golem (0.5 ms), the raid and patrol ticks, light, fluids. The villager brain itself (`VL-BRAIN`) is 1 percent once the scan is done.

Client (standard world, release, view 4, mean CPU microseconds a frame over 100 to 300 frames, the frame is CV-STEP: the core tick, packets, client tick, mesh queue, screen model, render list; the window submit needs a window and is not measured): village 26835 (base) and 20322 (this lane; the idle MESH-DIRTY after the frame averaged 4428 and 6912 us: a few dense sections re-meshed inside the 100 frames), 10283 over 300 frames with one re-mesh; field 8344 (base) and 13922 (this lane; 82 entity rows against 50: the mobs of a run are not the same). The sections near the village mesh no dearer than the others (average 5.0 ms near, 6.2 ms far; the dearest five, 105 to 130 ms for 10000 to 14000 vertices, are the leaf-heavy tree sections of both scenes, not village buildings): the client has no village specific per frame cost; what the operator sees as hitches in the village is the 100 ms re-mesh of a dense section after a block change (max step 273 ms base, 215 ms this lane, 64 ms in the 300 frame run), which is the mesher's own cost (lane perfframe).
