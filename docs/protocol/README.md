# Protocol 777 interoperability specification

This is a written specification of the network protocol and data formats used by Java Edition 26.3.
The original voxel game built in this repository uses this protocol as its network layer. It is written in
prose and tables only. It covers the plumbing (framing, packets, registry synchronization, chunk encoding, NBT and
region files) and, from document 10 on, the behaviour of the shipped game that the original game reproduces:
world generation, client rendering and controls, texture-only resource packs, commands, tools, weapons and combat,
mobs and, from document 22 on, the code-defined engines and registries that the standard world type needs (extraction
tool and registry loading, block and item registries, block behaviour, redstone, block entities, light, world
simulation, entity models, worldgen features, structures, the Nether, the End, portals, menus, item entities,
particles, tint sources, residual mob questions, entity NBT, registry ids, the default skin) and, in document 48, the shapes of the shipped data files. Document 50 covers the level-loading screen (spawn-area preparation, chunk-status grid, the join wait). Document 52 covers the block entity models of chests, shulker boxes and banners (and records that beds are plain block models) in part 1 and, in part 2, the rendering of signs and hanging signs (the sign board models and textures, the text plane transform per sign family, line layout, glow outline, colours, light, font, distance rules, the facing-to-rotation mapping and hit-box centres). Document 52 §8 covers how the End portal and the End gateway are drawn (slab and cube geometry, face rules, the layered screen-space portal shader with its tint table, pipeline state, the gateway beam). Document 53 covers the player movement poses (standing, crouching, swimming and crawling, sprinting) and their server and client handling. Document 55 covers the game-test framework behind the `/test` command (the subcommands, the `test_instance` and `test_environment` data, the runner, the test block and test instance block in a run, the packets and what gates it). Document 56 covers the superflat world generator, its presets and the create-world screens for it. Document 57 (five parts) closes client-side gaps left by earlier documents: rendering and animation details (the first-person fire overlay, camera bobbing and damage tilt, the piglin crossbow arms, the leather overlay layer), world data, sounds, save formats and brewing fuel, the Login packet layout, Level Particles options and fixture checks, the command node tree, and the client handling of chat, titles, the action bar, boss bars and scoreboards. Document 58 covers the held item while it is in use or charged: the use state of a living entity and the metadata flags that carry it (entry 8), the exact semantics of the item-definition properties the shipped definitions use (`using_item`, `use_duration`, `crossbow/pull`, `use_cycle`, `charge_type`, `display_context`, `compass`, `time` and the others), the goat horn's definition, models and display transforms, the third-person `TOOT_HORN` arm pose, and one table of every in-use item (bow, crossbow, shield, horn, trident, spears, brush, spyglass, food and drink, fishing rod) with models, display transforms and arm branches. Document 58 also corrects earlier documents in place: 12 §7.5 (the `SPYGLASS` and `TOOT_HORN` rows, whose left-arm yRot sign was wrong, and the idle-bob rule), 18 §1.4 (the sentence on entity data slots 15 and up) and 34-2 §4.2 (the arm order and the parenthetical list of arm-pose formulas); it adds pointer lines to 08 §6.3, 12 (§6.6, §6.13 and Open question 6), 25-7 §12.2 and 57-1 §3.4. Document 59 covers the movement of the local player tick by tick (tick order, input, walking, climbing, fluids, flight, collision response, the block effect pass, every block that changes movement, the movement effects and enchantments and the updates the server sends, fall distance and fall damage, and pushes) and corrects earlier documents in place: 13 §1, 18 §3.2, §3.3, §7.4 and §9.2, 30-8 §14.1, 53 §9.2, with additions to 05a 0x24, 05b 0x67, 0x87 and TeleportFlags, 17 §4.6 and open item 19, 24 parts 1, 5 and 8, 28 §10, 29a §8.3, 39 §2.2a, 42-7 §3.2, 44-1 §4.4 and 51 §6.1 and open question 8. Document 07 §10 also records the vanilla 26.3 save folder exactly as the game reads and writes it (`level.dat`, saved-data files, player files, dimension folders), so that a vanilla save can be opened and written back without loss. The game has two world
types. The **standard** world type is Minecraft 26.3 content: its data comes from a vanilla resource pack and data pack
that the extraction tool builds from the user's own jar (22, 23), and the engines are the documents of this folder.
The **cobolcraft** world type is the invented `cobolcraft:` content of [../design/standin-content.md](../design/standin-content.md)
with its hand-authored core ([../design/cobolcraft-standard-core.md](../design/cobolcraft-standard-core.md)). Gameplay rules are
shared. [../design/world-types.md](../design/world-types.md) defines the mechanism; [../design/README.md](../design/README.md)
is the source-of-truth map. Documents 10 to 21, 22 to 53, 55, 57, 58 and 59 were written from the artifacts below in clean-room rounds
(reader and reviewer agents, see `.claude/skills/clean-room/SKILL.md`); implementers work from these documents only.

## Conventions

- Documents 24 to 25, 29b, 30 to 31, 34 to 37, 40, 42, 44, 45, 52 and 57 are families split into numbered parts (`NN-name-K-topic.md`; the five parts of 57 each have their own row in the Documents table); a reference to a family without a part ("24") means the part that holds the topic, and part 1 of each family holds the shared model. Names ending `-x1`, `-x2` are continuations written after the numbered parts of the same family, to finish topics a part did not reach (25 x1, 40 x1, 44 x1 and x2). Document 29a is a sibling of 29, and 15a of 15.
- Packet ids are shown in hex and decimal. Within each phase and direction, a packet's id is the order in which it is registered.
- Fields marked "(inferred)" were deduced rather than read directly. Each document ends with an "Open questions" section, numbered per document ("Open question 5"); in a split family each part has its own list.
- **Recalled knowledge is not evidence.** Every value in documents 10 to 50, and in 59, was read from an artifact in its round. Anything not read is marked "(recalled, not verified)" or "(inferred)" and listed in the document's Open questions. See "Recalled knowledge is not evidence" in the clean-room skill.
- **Section references** between documents use the form "NN §x", for example "17 §4.4" or, for a split family, "24 part 6 §3" or "31-5 §2"; a reference such as "18 framework: <topic>" names a topic of document 18.
